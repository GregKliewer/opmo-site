The problem it solves

“We funded this and we are not getting it, and we cannot tell you whether the problem is the plan, the people, or the structure.” That sentence sits behind most stalled transformations: the milestones were hit and the outcome in the business case did not arrive.

Sharpest when an investment has landed and the results haven't: AI adoption at scale, a product-model transition, large-scale cloud adoption, a cloud divestment or repatriation, a portfolio or platform consolidation, an M&A integration, regulatory or mandate-driven change, or a post-reorg reset. Different triggers, one underlying failure: the structures that carry change were never re-cut when the mandate changed.

The ninth dimension

Every structural change identifies who stands to lose, what is lost, and names the decider. This dimension scores whether the person accountable for the change can actually afford to make it, given who they still need on their side the following Monday.

It’s the only dimension that has nothing to do with the quality of the plan, and it’s the one that most often explains why a good plan stopped moving.

Fixed scope, fixed deliverable set, fixed end date. The diagnostic stands alone: a document your leadership can act on whether or not you engage anyone for the work that follows. Contact me to scope it.

Where it leads

One hundred percent of the diagnostic fee is credited against whatever comes next, if commissioned within sixty days of the readout. The diagnostic names which of these the findings call for.

Option A

Team & Capacity Alignment

For the platform or delivery leader whose teams are absorbing new capability with no resourcing model behind it. Squad structures with capacity explicitly budgeted for the new work alongside the old, delivered as a pilot tranche and repeatable across the portfolio.

Option B

Operating Model Redesign

The full structural intervention when the diagnostic points above team level: decision rights, governance architecture, a product-model transition, or an integration operating model, delivered as a modular quarterly program with a go or no-go at each quarter boundary.

Option C

The Standing Seat

When the diagnostic finds that the work needs an owner rather than a plan, it points instead to the fractional seat described under how the engagement works.

I hold one fractional seat at a time, by design. That cap is what keeps room on the calendar for this kind of scoped, project-based work, rather than filling every day with retainer hours.
